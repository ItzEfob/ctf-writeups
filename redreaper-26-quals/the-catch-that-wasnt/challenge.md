# The Catch That Wasn't

**Category** Satellite 
**Difficulty** Hard

![The Catch That Wasn't cover](monkey.jpeg)

The monkey filing clerk found two August flight cards stapled to one recovery receipt. The earlier card describes a camera-less capsule retrieved after it reached the ocean. The later receipt says the capsule was caught before it touched the water.

An aircrew memoir offers a clue. The recovery plane almost stayed grounded while a cylinder was changed in its left engine. Fix the catalog card for the later flight.

## Your task

Identify the satellite mission behind the airborne recovery. Cross-check a declassified launch ledger, a firsthand aircrew account, and a contemporary American civil-space record for that mission. Begin with the [NRO's declassified programs archive](https://www.nro.gov/foia-home/foia-declassified-nro-programs-and-projects/) or the [CIA museum's program overview](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/exhibit/corona-americas-first-imaging-satellite-program/).

Submit these four fields in order.

1. The four-digit internal mission number in the launch ledger.
2. The word-and-number call sign used by the plane's crew on the successful airborne recovery.
3. The aircraft's short three-digit marking, including any leading zero.
4. The Greek-letter word in the contemporary designation on the civil-space record. Omit the year and any suffix.

Format your answer as `rxr{monkey_mission_callsign_aircraft_greekword}`. Use lowercase throughout, remove the space in the call sign, and put one underscore between fields. A browser and the public archives are sufficient.
